Hierarchical clustering definition:
    Hierarchical Clustering is an unsupervised learning algorithm that creates clusters by progressively 
    merging similar data points , forming a hierarchy of clusters.

There are two approaches in hierarchical clustering:
    1) Agglomerative HC(bottom-up)
    2) Divisive HC(top to bottom)

Agglomerative approach:
Here each observation is considered as the cluster of its own . Then the most simillar clusters are
successively merged until there is just one single big cluster represented as tree or dendogram.

steps:

step 1 : Make each data point a cluster that forms n cluster
step 2: take two closest data points and make them one cluster that forms n-1 cluster
step 3: repeat the step 2 until it forms big cluster

In hierarchical clustering , we use dendogram to identify and find the best 
optimised k value as we do in k-means clustering using elbow method.

The number of clusters that best represents the different groups can be chosen 
by observing the dendrogram.

the best choice of the no of clusters is the no of vertical lines in dendogram cut
by a horizontal line that can traverse the maximum distance vertically without intersecting
cluster or merging

we use several approches in Agglomerative clustering to find the best k:
they are single link , complete link , average link

in single link , we calculate distance between nearest points using minimum distance value between
    two points , it forms chaining effect and no clear seperation of clusters 
    example AB paired and we are now linking with C 
    distance of(AB, C) is  min((A,C), (B,C)) we choose min
    if b is close to c then forms single cluster and chaining effects 
    in dendogram we cannot observe clearly the gap difference.

    but in Complete link , we choose max dis that covers the real distance and compact formation
    of cluster so we can observe clearly the gap between the merged clusters
    and we can decide the best k.
